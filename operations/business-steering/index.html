The problem

Lots of data.

No knowledge.

Most SMEs have more data than ever — in accounting, CRM, spreadsheets, the ERP. The problem isn't lack of data. It's that nobody reads, interprets and translates it into decisions systematically.

Business steering means knowing the right numbers, building simple reporting, and grounding decisions — on price, customers, processes, staff — in facts instead of feeling.

01

Decisions come too late

Whoever sees in the annual accounts that a product line was loss-making acts too late. Near-real-time steering numbers allow early intervention — while it still helps.

02

Discussions without facts

When the leadership team each has a different perception and no shared data, meetings become opinion rounds. Clear KPIs give every discussion a basis.

03

Growth without steering

Revenue grows, margin falls. Staff grows, productivity falls. Without metrics you notice too late. With them, you see it coming.

04

No lever for delegation

Delegating needs KPIs. Without measurable goals and reporting, the owner must control everything — and stays trapped in the daily operation.

FAQ

Which KPIs does an SME really need?

The most important for almost any SME: revenue and growth (month vs. prior year), contribution margin (total and per segment), a rolling liquidity forecast, order intake and pipeline, customer satisfaction, and staff productivity. That's 6–7 numbers that, read regularly, deliver 80% of the relevant steering information.

How do I build simple reporting?

In three steps: define the 5–8 most relevant KPIs from the steering dimensions, identify the data sources and owners, and introduce a weekly or monthly review rhythm — 30 minutes, the same numbers, the same structure. That's all it takes to start.

What's the difference between BI and normal reporting?

Normal reporting shows what happened. Business intelligence shows what it means and what follows. The difference isn't the software but the question. 'Revenue was €320k' is reporting. 'Revenue was €320k — 12% under plan, driven by segment B where three customers left citing the same reason' is business intelligence.

Which software do I need?

Less than most think. A well-structured spreadsheet often suffices to start. Power BI (free in many Office licenses) or similar offer visual dashboards without programming. Expensive isn't better — what counts is whether the numbers are read and interpreted regularly.
