The Pareto view

Not all revenue

is worth having.

The most uncomfortable insight in profit optimization: some of your customers cost you money. Some products are sold at a loss once true costs are counted. Most owners don't know which — because they look at revenue, not contribution margin.

The first step is always the same: which 20% of customers and products create 80% of the profit? And which 20% cause 80% of the effort without the reward? Once that's visible, the decisions become obvious.

01

Pricing

The fastest profit lever. A 5% price increase, cleanly communicated, often flows almost entirely to the bottom line. Most SMEs price too low out of fear — not calculation.

02

Cost structure

Not blanket cutting — targeted review. Which costs create value, which are habit? The 20% of costs that could be cut without pain.

03

Customer & product mix

Focus on the profitable 20%. Letting go of loss-making customers can raise profit while lowering revenue. That's not a paradox — that's Pareto.

04

Process efficiency

Every hour of rework, every avoidable error, every friction point is margin lost. Operational excellence and profit are the same lever from two sides.

FAQ

How can I improve margin without raising prices?

Several ways: focus on the profitable customer segment, cut loss-making products, reduce rework and errors, renegotiate purchasing, and review the cost structure for habit-based spending. But pricing remains the fastest lever — and most SMEs underprice out of fear rather than calculation.

What is contribution margin and why does it matter?

Contribution margin is revenue minus variable costs — what's left to cover fixed costs and profit. Knowing it per product and customer reveals which are actually worth having. Many SMEs steer by revenue and miss that their biggest customer is their least profitable.

Is it really possible to earn more with less revenue?

Yes — and it's more common than owners expect. By dropping loss-making customers and products and focusing on the profitable core, revenue can fall while profit rises. Fewer customers, less complexity, higher margin. It's the Pareto principle applied to the P&L.

How quickly does profit optimization show results?

Pricing and mix decisions can show effect within one to two billing cycles. A full margin analysis with cost review and repricing typically takes 30–90 days. The QuickWin workshop identifies the fastest lever in half a day.
