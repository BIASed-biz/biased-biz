Why SMEs lose talent

It's rarely the salary.

It's the impression.

01

No employer profile

Candidates can't tell why they'd want to work for you rather than the competitor. A clear employer positioning — what you stand for, who fits — changes that.

02

A slow, unclear process

Good candidates have multiple offers. A recruiting process that takes weeks and communicates unclearly loses them to faster competitors — regardless of the actual job quality.

03

Invisible where they look

Talent searches on Google, LinkedIn, employer review sites. An SME invisible there isn't in the running — however good the workplace actually is.

04

Losing the people you have

The cheapest recruiting is keeping your team. High turnover is a symptom, not fate — and usually addressable once the causes are visible.

FAQ

How do I win employees as a small company?

Through positioning, not budget. Be clear about what you offer that a corporation can't: proximity to decisions, real influence, a visible impact of one's work, flexibility. Communicate it consistently — website, job ads, LinkedIn. And make the process fast and human. SMEs win on authenticity, not on perks.

Why do candidates leave during the process?

Usually because the process is too slow, too impersonal, or unclear about next steps. Good candidates have options and read a slow process as disinterest. Speed and clarity beat elaborate assessment stages for most SME roles.

Is employer branding worth it for SMEs?

Yes — but not as an expensive campaign. For SMEs, employer branding means being clear and visible about who you are and who fits. A consistent, honest presence outperforms a polished campaign that overpromises. Authenticity is the SME's advantage.

How do I reduce staff turnover?

First make the causes visible — exit conversations, honest feedback, turnover by team and role. Turnover clusters usually point to a specific problem: a manager, a role design, a broken process. Addressing the cause is cheaper than constantly re-recruiting.
