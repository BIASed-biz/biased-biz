FAQ

What is the Pareto principle (80/20 rule)?

The Pareto principle observes that in most situations, roughly 20% of causes create 80% of results. Named after economist Vilfredo Pareto, it appears everywhere: 20% of customers often generate 80% of profit, 20% of products most revenue, 20% of problems most disruption. The exact ratio varies, but the pattern — that results are unevenly distributed and concentrated in a vital few — holds remarkably widely.

How do I apply the 80/20 rule to my business?

Start by finding your specific vital few: which 20% of customers create most of your profit? Which products, which activities? Then act on it — focus resources on the vital 20% and deliberately reduce the trivial 80%. The hard part isn't the analysis; it's the courage to drop the low-return customers, products and activities that feel like they matter but don't. Applied honestly, it concentrates effort where it actually pays.

Why is Pareto a leadership discipline, not just a fact?

Because knowing 80/20 changes nothing; living it changes everything. Most people know the principle but still spread effort evenly across everything. The discipline is asking, constantly and about every decision: which few things here actually matter? — and having the courage to say no to the rest. That's a practice, not a piece of knowledge. The owners who internalize it work on what matters while others just work hard.

Can the Pareto principle be applied to my own time?

Yes — and it's the hardest, highest-value application. Ask: which 20% of your work as an owner creates 80% of your value to the business? Usually it's a small set of things only you can do — key decisions, key relationships, direction. Yet most owners spend the majority of their time on the trivial many that others could handle. Freeing yourself to focus on your vital few is often the single biggest leadership lever.
