FAQ

How can I get more out of my business without working more?

Look inward before outward. The largest untapped potential is usually already inside: prices set too low out of fear (correct them and profit rises immediately), existing customers you're underserving (sell them more of what they need), your own time wasted on trivial tasks (delegate and refocus), and effort spread too thin (sharpen focus). These release value you already have — no new customers, no extra hours required.

What is the hidden potential in an existing business?

It's the value you're producing but not capturing: the margin lost to underpricing, the sales left on the table with existing customers, the owner's high-value time consumed by low-value tasks, and the results diluted by lack of focus. Most businesses have significant hidden potential precisely because it's invisible — it doesn't show up as a problem, just as results quietly lower than they could be.

Is it better to get more customers or more from existing ones?

Usually more from existing ones first — it's cheaper, faster and lower-risk. Existing customers already trust you, so selling them more of what they need costs little. Correcting underpricing releases profit with no new customers at all. New-customer acquisition is valuable but expensive; exhaust the cheaper internal levers first, then grow externally from a stronger base.

How do I know what potential I'm missing?

The honest questions: Are my prices set by calculation or by fear? Am I selling my existing customers everything they'd value? Is my own time spent on what only I can do, or on tasks others could handle? Is my effort focused or spread thin? Most owners find significant answers in at least two of these. An outside perspective is valuable precisely because this potential is, by nature, hard to see from inside.
