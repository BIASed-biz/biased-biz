First priorities

In a crisis,

sequence is everything.

01

Secure liquidity first

Nothing else matters if the money runs out. A 13-week liquidity forecast and immediate cash levers come before any strategic question. Survival before optimization.

02

See the situation clearly

Crises breed panic and denial in equal measure — both distort reality. The first job is an honest, unsentimental picture of where things actually stand.

FAQ

When does a business need crisis consulting?

When the ability to act is threatened: liquidity is tight and worsening, a key customer or supplier is lost, losses are mounting, or a market shift has undercut the business model. The best time to seek help is early — while there are still options. Crisis consulting late, when insolvency looms, is harder and narrower.

What's the first step in a crisis?

Secure liquidity and see the situation honestly. Before any strategic decision, you need to know how much runway you have — a 13-week liquidity forecast — and an unsentimental picture of the real situation. Panic and denial both distort; clarity is the foundation of every good crisis decision.

Can a crisis be an opportunity?

Sometimes, genuinely. A crisis forces decisions that were overdue — dropping loss-making customers, fixing broken processes, sharpening focus. Businesses that survive a crisis well often emerge leaner and clearer. But that only happens with decisive action; a crisis is not an opportunity for those who freeze.

How fast can a crisis be stabilized?

Immediate liquidity measures can show effect within days to weeks. Stabilizing the situation and building a clear turnaround plan typically takes the first 30–90 days. The key is speed at the start: in a crisis, the cost of waiting compounds.
