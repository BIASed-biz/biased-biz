The best financing

is the one that fits your business.

Every founder asks: where does the money come from? But the better question is which kind of money fits — because each financing path shapes the company differently. Equity dilutes ownership; debt demands repayment; public funding takes time but is cheap. The wrong financing can constrain a good business for years.

The seven paths

Seven ways to finance

a business in Germany.

01

Bootstrapping

Finance from own means and early revenue. Keeps full control and forces discipline — but limits growth speed. The right choice more often than founders think.

02

Bank loan / KfW

Classic debt, often via house bank with KfW backing. Cheap and non-dilutive, but requires collateral or a convincing plan — and repayment regardless of success.

03

EXIST founder grant

Public funding for university-linked tech and knowledge-based startups. Covers living costs and materials during the early phase. Non-dilutive — but application-intensive.

04

Business angels

Private investors bringing capital plus experience and network. Good for the early phase — the right angel is worth more than their money. Dilutive.

05

Venture capital

Institutional equity for high-growth, scalable models. Significant capital — but with strong growth expectations and loss of some control. Fits few businesses, not most.

06

Grants & subsidies

Federal, state and EU programs for specific purposes — innovation, digitalization, regional development. Cheap money, but bureaucratic and slow. Worth it for the patient.

07

Revenue-based & alternatives

Crowdfunding, revenue-based financing, factoring, leasing. Situational tools that fit specific needs — often overlooked, sometimes exactly right.

FAQ

What is the EXIST founder grant?

EXIST is a German federal program supporting startups emerging from universities and research institutions, particularly in technology and knowledge-based fields. It funds founders' living costs and startup materials during the early development phase. It's non-dilutive — you keep full ownership — but the application is demanding and requires a university affiliation.

How does KfW startup financing work?

KfW is Germany's state development bank. It doesn't lend directly to founders but provides favorable-rate loans through your house bank, often with partial risk relief for the bank. This makes financing accessible for founders who might not get a standard bank loan. Programs like the ERP founder loan are common starting points.

Should I take equity or debt financing?

It depends on your model. Debt (loans) keeps ownership but demands repayment regardless of success — suited to businesses with predictable cash flow. Equity (angels, VC) shares risk but dilutes ownership and control — suited to high-growth, uncertain models. Many solid SMEs are better served by debt or bootstrapping than by chasing VC that doesn't fit.

How much financing does a startup really need?

Often less than founders assume. The instinct to raise a large round can be a trap — more money invites less discipline and more dilution. Calculate the actual runway to the next meaningful milestone, add a buffer, and raise that. Bootstrapping to profitability is underrated and keeps all options open.
