FAQ

What is KDD (Knowledge Discovery in Databases)?

KDD is the process of finding useful, non-obvious patterns in data — relationships and insights that exist in your data but that nobody would spot by looking at it directly. For an SME, this often means discovering which customer characteristics predict profitability, what patterns precede churn, or which product combinations sell together. It turns raw data into decision-relevant knowledge that gut feeling alone would never reveal.

Why does knowledge management matter for SMEs?

Because in an SME, critical knowledge often lives in just a few people's heads — how a key process really works, why a customer stays, what was learned from past mistakes. When those people leave, retire or fall ill, the knowledge leaves with them, undocumented and unrecoverable. Knowledge management turns fragile individual knowledge into durable organizational knowledge — reducing dependence on any single person.

How do I secure knowledge before an employee leaves?

Document the critical, tacit knowledge while the person is still there: how key processes actually work (not the official version), important customer relationships and their history, and hard-won lessons. Build it into accessible systems — a master process, documented workflows, shared records. The best time is before notice is given; the second-best is now, systematically, for your key roles.

Do I need special software for knowledge management?

Usually not at the start. Knowledge management is more about discipline than software — capturing what matters, structuring it so others can find and use it, keeping it current. A well-organized shared system often suffices for an SME. The failure mode isn't lack of software; it's that nobody makes documenting and structuring knowledge a habit. Fix the habit before buying a tool.
