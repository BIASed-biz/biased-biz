Gut feeling vs. facts

Gut feeling is fast.

Facts are right more often.

01

Which numbers actually matter

Not all data is worth reading. The skill is finding the 5–8 numbers that genuinely inform your decisions — and ignoring the rest. More dashboards is not more insight.

02

Read on a rhythm

Data read once a year in the annual accounts steers nothing. The same numbers read weekly or monthly turn into a steering instrument. Rhythm, not volume, creates insight.

03

From number to decision

A number is not a decision. 'Margin fell 3%' is data; 'margin fell because segment B is underpriced, so we reprice' is a decision. The value is in the interpretation, not the figure.

04

The Pareto of data

Which 20% of customers create 80% of profit? Which 20% of products? The most valuable data insights are almost always Pareto patterns — and most SMEs have never looked.

FAQ

What data should an SME track?

Fewer numbers than most think — the 5–8 that genuinely inform decisions. For most SMEs: revenue and growth, contribution margin (total and by segment), liquidity forecast, order pipeline, customer satisfaction, and staff productivity. The skill isn't collecting more data — it's choosing the few numbers that matter and reading them regularly. More dashboards create noise, not insight.

How do I make decisions based on data instead of gut feeling?

Start small: pick the 5–8 numbers most relevant to your business, establish where they come from, and set a regular rhythm to review them — weekly or monthly, same numbers, same structure. Then practice translating each number into a decision. Gut feeling isn't wrong, but data-informed gut feeling is far more often right, especially as stakes rise.

Do I need expensive software for data-driven decisions?

No. A well-structured spreadsheet often suffices to start. The value isn't in the software but in choosing the right numbers and reading them regularly. Tools like Power BI (free in many Office licenses) add visual dashboards without programming. Expensive systems can even hurt — they create the illusion of insight while the numbers still go unread.

What's the difference between data and insight?

Data is what happened; insight is what it means and what to do about it. 'Revenue was €320k' is data. 'Revenue was €320k — 12% under plan because three customers in segment B left for the same reason, which we can fix' is insight. Most SMEs have plenty of data and little insight, because nobody does the interpretation.
