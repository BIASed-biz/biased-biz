Beyond the hype

The question is not 'AI or not'.

It's 'where would it actually help?'

01

Automate the repetitive

AI's clearest SME value is removing repetitive knowledge-work: drafting standard documents, summarizing, sorting inquiries, answering routine questions. The busywork that eats hours but needs no judgment.

02

Not everything, deliberately

AI is not the answer to every problem, and applying it everywhere wastes effort. The Pareto question: which one or two tasks, if automated, would free the most time for the least cost?

03

Cost and reality

AI has real costs — tools, setup, learning, oversight. And real limits — it makes confident mistakes. A clear-eyed view of both prevents the expensive disappointment of over-expectation.

04

Human judgment stays

AI handles the repetitive; humans handle the judgment, the relationships, the decisions. The businesses that win with AI use it to free human time for human work — not to replace what shouldn't be replaced.

FAQ

Is AI worth it for a small business?

For specific tasks, often yes — but not as a blanket transformation. The clearest value for SMEs is automating repetitive knowledge-work: drafting standard documents, summarizing information, sorting and answering routine inquiries. The question isn't whether to 'adopt AI' but which one or two specific, repetitive tasks it could take off your plate. Applied narrowly and deliberately, it saves real time; applied as hype, it wastes money.

What can AI actually do for an SME?

Practically: draft and edit routine text, summarize long documents or email threads, answer frequently-asked customer questions, sort and categorize inquiries, and assist with research. What it can't reliably do: exercise judgment, own relationships, make strategic decisions, or be trusted without oversight — it makes confident mistakes. Use it for the repetitive, not the critical.

How much does AI cost for a small business?

Less than the hype implies for basic use — many capable AI tools cost modest monthly subscriptions. The real costs are often setup, learning, and oversight rather than the tool itself. The mistake is either over-investing in AI as a transformation, or ignoring cheap tools that would save hours. A clear assessment of the specific task and its return prevents both errors.

Will AI replace my employees?

For most SMEs, no — and framing it that way misses the point. AI is better understood as removing busywork from your existing people, freeing them for the judgment, relationships and decisions that need a human. The businesses that benefit use AI to make their people more effective, not to replace them. The real risk isn't AI replacing workers — it's competitors using it to free time you're still spending on busywork.
