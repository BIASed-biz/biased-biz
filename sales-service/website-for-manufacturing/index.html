FAQ

What does a manufacturing website need?

Technical specifics, not marketing gloss. B2B buyers in manufacturing need precise capability data — machines, tolerances, materials, part sizes, capacity — plus certifications (ISO etc.) and proof of capability through reference parts and industries served. And a frictionless RFQ process. Buyers are comparing suppliers on capability; the website that documents capability clearly wins the request for quote.

How is a B2B manufacturing website different?

It sells on capability, not emotion. The visitor is a technical professional evaluating whether you can produce their specific part to spec, reliably, at the needed volume. That means precise technical data, visible certifications, and evidence of relevant experience matter far more than lifestyle imagery or emotional messaging. Precision and proof convert; polish alone doesn't.

What is an RFQ and how do I get more?

An RFQ — request for quote — is a buyer asking you to price a specific job. It's the key conversion for a manufacturer. To get more: document your capabilities precisely so qualified buyers self-identify, show certifications and reference work to build confidence, and make the RFQ submission effortless — clear what specs to send, easy to send them. The lower the friction, the more RFQs arrive.
