The Pareto of marketing

Most marketing budget

is spread too thin to work.

01

Find where your customers are

Not where the trend is. A B2B machine-shop and a local beauty salon need completely different channels. The first question isn't 'which channel' but 'where does my specific customer actually look?'

02

Focus beats spread

One channel done well beats five done poorly. A focused budget reaches enough people often enough to work; the same budget split five ways reaches nobody enough to matter.

03

Measure the return

Marketing you can't measure is a gamble. The channels worth keeping are the ones where you can trace spend to result — and cut the ones you can't justify.

04

Match the message to intent

Someone searching for your service is ready to buy; someone scrolling social media is not. The same message fails in one context and works in the other. Channel and message must fit together.

FAQ

Which online marketing channel is best for SMEs?

There's no universal best — it depends entirely on where your customers are. A local service business often gets the most from local SEO and Google. A B2B firm may do better with targeted content and LinkedIn. A visual, consumer-facing business might win on Instagram. The mistake is following the trend rather than the customer. Find where your specific customer looks, and start there.

How much should an SME spend on online marketing?

Less on more channels is the common mistake. Better to fully fund one channel that reaches your customers than to sprinkle budget across five that each reach nobody effectively. Start with enough to properly test one focused channel, measure the return, and scale what works. Marketing budget should be an investment you can trace, not a cost you hope helps.

Is social media worth it for every business?

No. Social media works for businesses whose customers are there and whose offer suits the format — visual, consumer-facing, community-driven. For many B2B or highly local businesses, the same effort in local SEO or direct outreach returns far more. Being on social media because everyone is, rather than because your customers are, wastes effort.

How do I know if my marketing is working?

By measuring the path from spend to result: how many leads, at what cost, converting at what rate, into how much revenue. If you can't trace a channel to results, you can't manage it — you're gambling. The channels worth keeping are the ones where the numbers justify the spend. The rest should be cut or fixed.
